# Runtime architecture

Junction guarantees one thing above all: an accepted intent runs at most once at a time, only for the one consumer that owns it, only while that consumer is ready and the intent's account is the current one, and it reports an honest outcome. This page describes the parts that keep that guarantee and names the test that guards each rule. Test files live in `packages/core/src` unless another path is named.

## Owners

Every piece of state has one owner. Only `Junction`, `JunctionError`, `intent`, `createMockSource`, `createMockStorage`, `testIncomingSource` and `testInboxStorage` are exported.

| Owner                                    | Responsibility                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `Junction`                               | The facade. Reads the options once, owns the lifecycle (idle, started, disposed), wires the owners below and turns observations and dispatches into admissions.                                            |
| `RouteTable`                             | The pure half of admission: compiled URL patterns, origin ownership, route matching, the payload, validation and the canonical copy. It also answers `inspect`.                                            |
| `Inbox`                                  | The records in admission order, the limits, the interaction ledger, the registrations, the scheduler turn, the one execution slot, the timers and the published entries.                                   |
| `Attempt`                                | One lease: its id, the account it started under, its signal, its commit latch and its execution object.                                                                                                    |
| `IdentityBinding`                        | The subscription to the application's identity store and the identity it last read.                                                                                                                        |
| `SourceLifetime`                         | One attachment: staging pushes made during `attach`, the launch read and its timeout, the source's status, and silence after disposal.                                                                     |
| `Diagnostics`                            | The lazy snapshot and the payload-free timeline, assembled only while someone listens.                                                                                                                     |
| `ValueStore`                             | An observable value. A notification pass stops as soon as a newer value lands.                                                                                                                             |
| `StorageWriter`                          | The one writer: snapshot writes serialized and coalesced, operation ids kept across a retry of an unchanged snapshot, the barriers admissions, leases and `flush()` wait on, and the storage's durability. |
| `parseSnapshot`, `getRestoredInbox`      | The pure half of restoration: every stored field checked and copied before it is trusted, then each intent restored through its definition's `persist.schema` or quarantined.                              |
| `createMockSource`, `testIncomingSource` | The test double, well behaved by default and able to misbehave on purpose, and the contract every source keeps.                                                                                            |

## The path of an observation

1. A source pushes through its sink. A push from a disposed or failed attachment is ignored and answers `disposed`; a push made while `attach` runs is staged and admitted in order once it returns.
2. The input is copied into bounded, frozen JSON (64 KiB, depth 16), so no route or consumer holds an alias into an SDK object.
3. A URL whose origin another source's routes own is ignored as `owned-by-other-source`. Every route of the source is evaluated; none is `no-route`, two are `ambiguous-route`.
4. The one route's `payload` builds the candidate, the intent's schema validates it, and the output is copied into a frozen canonical payload of at most 16 KiB. The account selector names the target of an account-bound intent.
5. The ledger turns a repeated occurrence key into a duplicate of the known intent, or a conflict when the payload differs. Without a key, every observation is a new intent.
6. The deadline is fixed from the receipt time, the moment the launch read started for a launch intent, and the record waits in the inbox.

## The path of an intent

Every change (an admission, a registration, an update, an identity change, a settlement, a timer) schedules one turn in a microtask. A turn:

1. Ends every waiting intent past its deadline, and invalidates the attempt of a running one.
2. Evaluates the waiting intents in admission order: selection first (primaries, then fallbacks, ambiguity held), then the owner's readiness, then the account. The first eligible intent is leased when the slot is free; every other one records why it waits.
3. Publishes the inbox, then invokes the leased handler with the handler it has at that moment.

The slot is held until the handler's promise settles, even after its attempt was invalidated, so a handler that ignores its signal never runs beside another. The settlement follows a fixed table: a committed effect wins over everything after it; an invalidated attempt is interrupted, expired or cancelled; a current one is acknowledged, rejected, retried or failed by what it answered.

## The path of a persistent intent

1. `start()` attaches the sources, then reads the storage. Admissions stage until the stored inbox is restored, then are admitted in order with their receipt times, clamped to the snapshot's; the clock ratchet starts at the snapshot's timestamp.
2. An admission is decided in memory and answers `committed` once the write holding it landed. The writer runs one write at a time, and every write carries every earlier change.
3. A lease holds the slot while its write lands. Then the handler runs only if the write committed, the attempt is still current and selection still picks its owner; otherwise the intent waits again with its attempt undone, or ends when it was cancelled or expired, since no handler ran.
4. A settlement is applied in memory at once, and its write follows. A failed write leaves the storage unconfirmed; persistent intents wait with `storage` until a write, from `flush()` or the next change, succeeds.

## Principles

1. Junction normalizes ownership and delivery, not routing, attribution or authentication.
2. A source translates one provider SDK onto the contract and nothing more. The core never branches on an adapter's name.
3. The application owns its SDK. It initializes, configures and grants consent with it; Junction only listens.
4. Constructing a Junction does nothing. Consuming or observing it creates no native demand.
5. Ownership comes before readiness. A blocked owner is never bypassed, and ambiguity is never resolved by mount order.
6. Equal URLs and payloads are not the same interaction. Only occurrence evidence deduplicates.
7. An execution belongs to its lease. Once the lease is invalidated, it can settle nothing.
8. Junction never throws into its host at runtime. Only misconfiguration throws; operations answer with results.
